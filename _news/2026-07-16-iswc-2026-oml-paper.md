I'm happy to say that our paper **['Bridging the Semantic Web and Model-Based Systems Engineering with the Ontological Modeling Language'](/assets/publications/Elaasar2026%20-%20Bridging%20the%20Semantic%20Web%20and%20Model-Based%20Systems%20Engineering%20with%20the%20Ontological%20Modeling%20Language.pdf)** was accepted at the [International Semantic Web Conference (ISWC 2026)](https://iswc-conferences.org). The paper is by Maged Elaasar (Modelware), me, Eduard Kamburjan (IT University of Copenhagen and University of Oslo), Mohammad Hamdaqa (Polytechnique Montréal), and Abdelwahab Hamou-Lhadj (Concordia University).

As in the title, the paper is intended to bridge the semantic web (SW) and model-based systems engineering (MBSE) communities. While SW principles have been used in MBSE for some time, we still see challenges. The paper thus has **three main points**:

1. **identify** these challenges. First, SE models lack semantics suitable for ontological reasoning. Languages such as OWL provide these semantics, but are not the proper level of abstraction for describing SE. Second, there is a lack of robust ontological modelling tools for engineers, and so no established methodology for using ontologies in SE.
2. **discuss** how OML and the openCAESAR framework address these challenges
3. **report** on how OML and openCAESAR are used in practice, with some discussion of reported benefits and drawbacks

We hope that this work is useful to the semantic web community in understanding the perspectives and requirements of the systems engineering community, as discussed below. We look forward to discussions about what needs to change on both sides such that the power of the semantic web can help in the development of complex systems.

An author pre-print is [available here](/assets/publications/Elaasar2026%20-%20Bridging%20the%20Semantic%20Web%20and%20Model-Based%20Systems%20Engineering%20with%20the%20Ontological%20Modeling%20Language.pdf). Related work of ours includes [openCAESAR: Balancing Agility and Rigor in Model-Based Systems Engineering](/publications/#Elaasar2023opencaesar) on the toolchain, and a [survey of practitioner experiences in model-based systems engineering](/publications/#Elaasar2025). OML itself, with tutorials, is at [opencaesar.io](https://www.opencaesar.io/oml/).

### Two challenges for the semantic web in systems engineering

We claim that 'the languages and frameworks currently used for ontology engineering are not suited to be integrated in MBSE workflows'. In the abstract, we identify two challenges: 'the lack of rigorous semantics for SE models, and the lack of a well-established methodology for effectively using ontologies in SE'. In the paper, we look at why each piece is still missing:

1. **Abstraction mismatch.** SE models lack **rigorous semantics** in the semantic web sense, so they cannot directly benefit from ontological reasoning. SysMLv2's semantics are defined internally by its meta-model, so reasoning over a SysML model means first generating a knowledge graph from it. Ontological languages such as OWL do provide these semantics, but are **not the proper level of abstraction** for describing SE.
   - Engineers wish to rely on well-known patterns (composition, aggregation), which OWL has no built-in relations for.
   - They also wish to reason about their system in a closed-world form, with a clear interface to an open-world.
2. **Inadequate tooling.** There is a **lack of robust ontological modelling tools** suited for experts used to textual and modular languages, and without them there is no **well-established methodology** for using ontologies in an engineering workflow.
   - "Protégé requires significant experience in knowledge representation and semantics".
   - Tools work on OWL axioms in isolation, so connecting constraints, queries, and mappings to an ontology means maintaining those dependencies by hand.
   - Other tooling requires alignment with upper ontologies, which can hamper the velocity of system development.

We thus argue in the paper that OML provides **a suitable layer above OWL**, such that engineers can have the benefits of the semantic web (interoperability, ontological reasoning) while still matching MBSE practice requirements (modularity, textual syntax), and that openCAESAR provides suitable tooling for practice (CI/CD, Maven versioning, textual editor).

### OML in practice

We report on a few examples of OML and openCAESAR use in practice. For example, at JPL, an ontology was created in OML to provide consistency and semantic checks for spacecraft electrical wiring. This replaced an **error-prone Excel spreadsheet**. At JAXA, OML was used as a semantic backbone for spacecraft orbital analysis, shrinking the time required for analysis **from weeks to hours**. At Leonardo UK, OML vocabularies were used as the common ontology to bridge the data formats and semantics of separate avionics tools.

In academia, we mention the work by others on modelling validity frames, and our own work on using OML in the modelling of Digital Twins, such as [DTInsight](/publications/#Fiter2025).
